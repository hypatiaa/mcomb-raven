What it is. Two kinds of exact walks. Clip walks are the closed Eisenstein walks that bound the bird, its five tone bands and the eye; they are certified closed (integer check) and are never drawn, they only say where tone strokes may go. Ink lines are open fine walks of cyclotomic phasors (unit teeth ζ^q, integer q, 10080 directions): the smooth hard edges (beak ridge, mandible, talons, branch) and interior ridge lines. Feathered edges get no outline: feather tips taper into the paper. The tones are open strokes laid along the hatching direction of the drawing, like pen work: comb arcs (linear phase) whose length follows how coherent the plumage direction is, cross-hatched at 60° steps for the dark tones, delicate highlight strokes over the whole bird, and open curlicues (a discrete Euler spiral, turn A·k between phasors) as accents in the darkest areas. Nothing open has to close, by design. Epicycles were always a kludge; these are cyclotomic phasors. The cyan arrows in the inset are the comb window, the last m phasors before the pen tip; the amber chord is their resultant.

Why it matters. Closure of the clip walks is certified in exact integer arithmetic, every phasor is an integer phase, and the file is small.

Credits. Instrument: mcomb (cyclotomic phasors). Curlicue idea: Gemini (vision seat). Program: Opus (Claude Opus 5.5).

Limits. The tracing is numerical (thresholds, a difference of Gaussians, thinning, a structure tensor). The interior ridge lines are few, because the source's fine hatching breaks them into short pieces. Spacing, alpha, width and the extra cross-hatching and highlight families are drawing-register choices. The light area under the belly is enclosed paper.

Tracing: numerical (from the drawing). Phasors, vertices, closure, tone strokes: exact integers in ℤ[ζ₆] and ℤ[ζ₁₀₀₈₀]. Drawing: floating point (drawing register).
